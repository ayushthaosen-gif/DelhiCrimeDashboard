Delhi road safety: where to look first

A one-page brief for planners. It points to places the Traffic Police crash reports keep naming, so a field audit can start there. It does not say why a crash happens or what should be built.

What the reports show

  • 81 of 323 named crash-prone places appear in five or more of the nine annual lists (2016 to 2024); 35 appear in seven or more.
  • Ring Road, Outer Ring Road and GTK Road carry the most: 197 of 366 fatal crashes counted in the 2023 zone list were on these three roads (Ring Road 69, Outer Ring Road 64, GTK Road 64).
  • In 2024, 51 of the 93 named zones are in evidence tiers 1 and 2 and hold 222 of the 375 fatal crashes counted in the zones. 20 zones are pedestrian crash-prone, with 71 pedestrian fatal crashes counted in them.

On the ground

  1. Agree the exact location with the Traffic Police first; positions are hand-placed estimates.
  2. Record where people actually cross, whether crossings and signals exist and work, speeds, lighting, sight lines and bus-stop placement.
  3. Note what the data cannot show: traffic volume, signal timing, enforcement, informal crossings.
  4. Repeat the same observations after any change, at the same places.

What this does not tell you

  • Why a place has crashes, who is responsible, or what to build.
  • Risk: there are no traffic or pedestrian counts, so counts cannot become rates.
  • Whether a missing crossing or signal is absent or just not mapped: for 56 of 93 zones nothing is mapped nearby.
  • A change over time: each year selects its own zones, so a changing count is not a trend at fixed places. A repeated name is not a proven identical footprint.
