Data & methods

Original sources, definitions, how geography changed, downloads and how to cite or reproduce a view. Everything in Explore is built from the files described here.

Can I compare these years?

Having a value for every year does not make a valid trend: a change can come from how the data was collected, defined or mapped rather than from what happened. Pick a measure and two years to check.

Comparable: a change figure is shown. Compare with caution: shown with a warning. Not comparable: both values stay visible, but the change is not calculated and trend lines are not joined. Unavailable: left blank, never zero. Rules live in data/continuity.json.

How current is each series?

Official crime and crash figures stop at 2024. Newer material exists but is kept off the maps on purpose. Never compare a year-to-date or provisional series with a full year.

Ward-level layers use the legacy 290-ward geography (before the 2022 delimitation), not the current 250 MCD wards. Source: data/series_status.json.

How figures are calculated

Rates: per km² and per 1 lakh residents

count ÷ district area (km²) or count ÷ population × 100,000. Density stops bigger districts being flattered for having more of everything. Population is approximate and excludes daytime visitors.

Rank and percent vs. average

Rank is a district's position when all districts with a figure are sorted descending; 1st is the highest. Percent vs. average is (district − mean) ÷ mean × 100.

Map colours

Districts are grouped into five bands of three by rank within the selected year, so one outlier can't wash out the scale. Colours are not comparable between years.

Correlation (r) and confidence

Pearson r between an infrastructure density and a crime density, using only districts with real coverage for that infrastructure. With 9 or 15 districts, r has wide uncertainty: treat it as suggestive, never as cause and effect. A positive r for police stations most likely reflects stations being sited where crime and population already are.

OpenStreetMap layers

Overbridges, bus stops, ATMs, liquor shops, metro gates and CCTV/guard posts are community-mapped. A zero means nothing was mapped, not that nothing exists. Where mapping is thin, r measures mapping effort as much as infrastructure.

Glossary

Registered case
A First Information Report recorded by police. Reflects reporting and registration, not only crime.
IPC / BNS
Indian Penal Code, replaced by the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita on 1 July 2024.
SLL
Special and Local Laws, such as the Arms Act, NDPS Act and Excise Act.
Crash-prone zone
A location on the Traffic Police blackspot list for that year's report.
Fatal crash vs. persons killed
A fatal crash has at least one death; one crash can kill several people.
Police vs. traffic district
Delhi Police uses 15 districts; some Traffic Police tables use 11 reporting districts.
PAPL survey
Streetlight and underpass survey covering 9 of 15 districts.
OSM
OpenStreetMap, a community-mapped inventory, not an official register.
